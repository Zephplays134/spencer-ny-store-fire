Was the Spencer, New York store fire by accident?

Undetermined

Nobody has said yes or no yet. The Shurfine Food Mart, the village's only grocery store, burned down on Friday, September 11, 2026. Officials have not announced a cause: it is still under investigation, so there is no confirmed answer one way or the other.

Last checked: October 28, 2026. No ruling of accident or arson has been released.

How it happened

  • ~12:30 p.m. Sept 11 — A deli employee spotted black smoke coming from the loading dock area. He told someone to call 911 and got everyone out of the building.
  • That afternoon — Fire crews from across the region responded. Firefighters said the old, dry timbers let the fire spread fast once it reached the ceiling.
  • Same evening — Demolition began hours after the flames were out. The building came down the same day it burned.
  • Saturday, Sept 12 — Spencer firefighters returned to the site and spent several hours putting out remaining hot spots.
  • Sept 13 — The Village of Spencer posted a public letter calling the loss deeply significant for the whole community.
  • October — The investigation is still open. As of Oct 28, no cause has been announced.

Why it matters here

The Shurfine was Spencer's only full-service grocery store, and a community landmark for decades under names including Possingers and the Big M. Losing it means the nearest full grocery run is now about 25 to 30 minutes in any direction. A Dollar General remains in the village, but residents say it is no substitute: staples like milk and eggs have been selling out since the fire, and not everyone can drive to Ithaca or order groceries online. The Spencer-Van Etten school district also had to adjust that day, canceling planned safety drills while classes continued near the fire scene.

Common questions

  • Was anyone hurt? No. Employees got out safely and no injuries were reported.
  • Was it arson? Unknown. Officials have ruled out nothing and ruled in nothing; the investigation is open.
  • Who owned the store? It was independently owned and had served the village for decades, previously as the Big M and before that Possingers.
  • Where can people shop now? A Dollar General in the village for basics, plus food-sharing cabinets around Spencer, food cupboard distributions, and the Food Bank of the Southern Tier directory. Full grocery runs now take about 25 to 30 minutes.
  • Was the school affected? Spencer-Van Etten Middle School is a few blocks away. Classes went on, but the district canceled that day's safety drills.
  • How can I help? The GoFundMe for the store's employees and the local food-sharing cabinets are the main channels.
  • When will we know the cause? There is no announced timeline. Law enforcement on scene said more information would be released later.

Since the fire

  • Neighbors expanded the local food-sharing cabinet network, adding a cabinet at the Inspire SV-E Community & Fitness Center at 57 E. Tioga St., closer to where the store stood. Requests have risen since the fire, including diapers and baby food.
  • The Spencer-Van Etten Food Cupboard, which serves about 220 to 240 families a month, lost a food source it had relied on the store for; displaced employees are being connected with the state Department of Labor.
  • A GoFundMe was set up to support the store's employees, who lost their jobs overnight.
  • Roughly 5,900 people in Tioga County already faced food insecurity in 2025 (about 1 in 8 residents), and local pantries report first-time visitors since the fire.
